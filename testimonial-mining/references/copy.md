# Hooks, captions and ad copy

Three pieces of writing per winner: the on-screen hook, the caption, and — for retargeting ads
only — the copy that sits above the video.

**One rule before any of them.**

> **You cut. You do not write the client's lines.**
>
> Every spoken word in a clip is a word the client actually said, in the order they said it.
> Trimming a stumble is editing. Rewriting a sentence into something cleaner they did not say is
> fabrication, and it is the one failure that turns a testimonial into a liability.
>
> **The audio is theirs. The on-screen text and the copy are yours.**

---

## 1. The on-screen hook

**Under ten words. Readable within a second. The same words they are about to hear.**

The first frame has to stop the scroll on its own — the viewer is thumbing past at speed with the
sound off, and the frame is the entire ad until they stop.

### The rules

- **Never open mid-sentence**, and never pull a line from the middle of an answer to use as the
  frame. If the hook line is not what the clip opens on, the clip is cut wrong — go back and move
  the in-point.
- **It is not a summary.** It is the words about to be spoken. A summary tells the viewer they
  already know what is coming, which is a reason to keep scrolling.
- **It leaves out the "how".** The hook opens a loop; the clip pays it off. A hook that answers
  its own question has no reason to be watched.
- **A different hook on every clip in the batch.** The hook is the variable being tested, so five
  clips opening the same way is one test run five times.

### The five patterns that work for a testimonial clip

Every example below is placeholder-shaped on purpose — **fill them from the user's own transcript,
never from a figure you invented or borrowed.**

| Pattern | Shape | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **The number, said out loud** | `[$X] in [TIMEFRAME]` | The clip's proof is a figure. Strongest pattern there is |
| **The before-state, named** | `"I was [BEFORE STATE] for [TIMEFRAME]"` | The viewer is currently in that state |
| **The doubt, named** | `"I nearly didn't do it because [DOUBT]"` | A doubt-resolved clip. Converts sceptics |
| **The objection, quoted** | `"I thought [OBJECTION]"` | A Q&A placement. Say the objection in their words |
| **The turn** | `"Then [WHAT CHANGED]"` | The mechanism in the client's own words |

**When a clip serves two outputs, write two hooks over the same cut** — one framing it as proof
for the retargeting ad, one framing it as an answer for the Q&A. Same footage, two pieces of text.

## 2. The caption

For the confirmation-page proof block and for any screenshot filed beside it.

**Client name · the before · the after · the timeframe.**

That is the whole format, and all four parts are required. A result with no caption is a picture
of a number, and it persuades nobody.

```
[CLIENT NAME], [WHAT THEY DO] — [BEFORE] → [AFTER] in [TIMEFRAME]
```

- **Label verified numbers verified.** Once in the section intro: *"every number below is
  verified"*. Only say it if it is true of every one of them.
- **If the funnel is local, name the geography in the header.** Relevance and trust.
- **A results disclaimer in the footer:** *"Results vary. Case studies are real client results but
  are not guaranteed."*
- Any part the user has not given you stays a visible bracket. `[TIMEFRAME]` in a draft is fine.
  A guessed timeframe in a published caption is not.

## 3. The ad copy above the video

Retargeting ads only. The proof block and the Q&A library do not take post copy.

### B2B — short, arrow bullets

```
[The result this client got, stated flat]

[CLIENT NAME] came to us [BEFORE STATE].

Here's what changed:
→ [What they were doing before]
→ [What they do now]
→ [The result, with the number]

[TIMEFRAME] later: [BEFORE] → [AFTER].

Click below to see whether it maps to your situation.
```

Revenue, ROAS, CAC, clients signed. **No lifestyle content in a B2B ad**, ever — not cars, not
travel, not the view from the desk.

### B2C — long-form, first person, story-led

It reads like a personal note, not a direct-response block. Written in the **founder's** voice
about the client, not in the client's voice — the client's voice is the video.

The shape, in order:

1. A disarming opening line about where this client started.
2. The open loop — the thing that was not what they thought it was.
3. Their exact cycle, in the words they used in the interview.
4. What changed, without teaching the mechanism.
5. The result, with the number and the timeframe.
6. The free video.
7. A warm sign-off with a first name.

**It never mentions a call, a price or an application.** The CTA points at the VSL page with a
**Learn More** button — never "comment", never "DM".

```
[Opening line about where [CLIENT NAME] was — the disarming one]

[The thing that wasn't what they thought]...

[Their exact cycle, in their words from the transcript]

[What changed — the turn, no mechanism taught]

[TIMEFRAME] later: [BEFORE] → [AFTER].

I made a free video breaking down how it works. Click below to watch.

Rooting for you,
- [FIRST NAME]
```

### For the Q&A placement instead of post copy

Write the **one-to-two-sentence written answer for the page**. Someone who reads only that and
never presses play should still have the objection handled — and it ends pointing forward to the
call, because an answer that closes the loop removes the reason to attend.

## 4. Before you hand it over

Check the batch against every line. These are not preferences.

- [ ] **Every number came from the user, or is a visible `[bracketed placeholder]`.** No rounding,
      no estimating, no filling in a likely figure.
- [ ] **Every quote is verbatim from the transcript.** Nothing paraphrased into something cleaner.
- [ ] **No clip opens mid-sentence.** If a hook does not match the first spoken words, the in-point
      is wrong.
- [ ] **Every hook in the batch is different.**
- [ ] **No hook closes its own loop.**
- [ ] **The client never delivers the CTA.** It is on-screen text or post copy.
- [ ] **The CTA matches the destination** — the VSL page in B2C, the booking or application page in
      B2B. No "comment KEYWORD" on a retargeting ad.
- [ ] **One idea per clip.** Two arguments means two clips.
- [ ] **No lifestyle content in a B2B ad.**
- [ ] **Every caption has all four parts** — name, before, after, timeframe.
- [ ] **Nothing claims "verified" that the user cannot evidence.**

**The read-it-aloud test.** Read every piece of copy out loud. If you would not say it in a
conversation, cut it. This is how AI slang gets caught — *"No stress. No fluff. Just results."*
Any tricolon of clipped negations is an instant credibility killer and must not survive into the
final draft. It is worse here than anywhere else in the funnel, because the whole asset is
selling authenticity.

Then list every remaining `[bracketed placeholder]` at the end of the file, so the user knows
exactly what they owe before anything gets published.
