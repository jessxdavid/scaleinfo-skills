# The three outputs

The reason mining an interview is affordable is that you are not running three content pipelines.
You are running one and pointing it at three places.

**A single client interview, filmed once, becomes:**

1. **A standing retargeting creative** — the most effective retargeting format in both SOPs, cut
   to **30–60 seconds**.
2. **A proof block on the confirmation page** — the client results section.
3. **An objection answer** — in the Q&A library on the page, and in the pre-call sequence.

Film once, cut three ways. Most people cut one ad out of an interview and never open the file
again, which is why their confirmation page has four testimonials on it and their Q&A library is
the founder answering their own objections.

---

## Output 1 — The retargeting ad

**The most effective retargeting format there is, in both the B2B and B2C SOPs.** Not one option
among five — the one that outperforms the others.

**Length: 30–60 seconds.** Longer is not more persuasive here; the audience already knows who the
user is, so there is nothing to establish.

### Where it runs

- **Budget: 15–20% of total ad spend.** This layer is funded out of that, not out of the cold
  budget.
- **Rotate 5–10 retargeting creatives monthly.** That cadence is the reason to mine interviews
  systematically rather than when someone remembers to.

**B2B audiences:** website visitors from the last 30 days who did not book, plus video viewers
(75%+) who did not visit.

**B2C audiences:** VSL page visitors who did not submit the form, VSL viewers (50%+) who did not
book, and 90-day engagers.

### The shape of the cut

| Beat | Seconds | What is in it |
|---|---|---|
| **First frame** | 0–1 | The on-screen hook. Stops the scroll on its own. Never mid-sentence |
| **The before** | 1–10 | Where they were, in their own words. The viewer is currently living here |
| **The turn** | 10–25 | What changed, or the doubt that resolved |
| **The after, with the number** | 25–45 | The specific. This is the clip's reason to exist |
| **CTA** | 45–60 | On screen and in the copy — the client does not deliver it |

**The client never delivers the call to action.** A testimonial that ends with the client telling
you to click stops being a testimonial. The CTA is on-screen text and the ad copy above the video.

**The CTA points where the funnel points.** In B2C that is the VSL page — "watch the free video".
In B2B it is the booking or application page. **Never a "comment KEYWORD" CTA** on a retargeting
ad; that sends paid traffic into a comment thread instead of the page that does the selling.

### The other retargeting creative types this footage feeds

Testimonial videos are the first of the list, but an interview usually also yields:

- **A case-study breakdown** — one client's journey with specific numbers. This is what a 3/3
  clip with a strong before-and-after becomes when there is enough material for a longer cut.
- **An objection-handling ad** — *"Tried things like this before? Here's what happened when…"*.
  Output 3 running in the standing layer instead of the pre-call layer.

Two types this footage will **not** produce, so do not force them: live proof / transparency
clips, and lifestyle / identity ads. Those are founder-filmed. Lifestyle is B2C-only in any case —
in B2B the proof is revenue, ROAS, CAC and clients signed, and running a lifestyle cut at
operators costs trust.

## Output 2 — The confirmation-page proof block

Where it lands: the **client results** section at the bottom of the confirmation page, alongside
the win screenshots.

This is the forgiving output. **No length ceiling, no scarcity, no bar to clear beyond being
real.** The clips that were too slow for an ad, the warm ones with no number, the ninety-second
answer that would not compress — all of them belong here.

> **The rule is literal: stack it so they cannot scroll to the bottom.** Not "add a few
> testimonials". Stack it until reaching the end of the page is not a realistic thing for a
> visitor to do, then keep stacking new wins and new interviews as they come in. **In B2C
> especially, the sheer volume of proof is what converts the sceptical.**

### What goes with each one

- **A caption: client name, the before, the after, the timeframe.** A result with no caption is a
  picture of a number.
- **Label verified numbers verified.** Say it once in the section intro and mean it.
- **If the funnel is local, name the geography in the header.** It buys relevance and trust.
- **A results disclaimer in the footer** — results vary, case studies are real client results but
  are not guaranteed.

Every new interview adds to this block. It is the only one of the three outputs where more is
straightforwardly better.

## Output 3 — The objection answer

Two homes, and the same cut serves both.

**Home 1: the Q&A library on the confirmation page.** One objection per video. **A client
answering an objection beats the founder answering it**, because the founder is the interested
party. Where a clip exists, use it instead of scripting a founder answer for that question.

**Home 2: beat 3 of the pre-call retargeting sequence.** The pre-call layer runs three beats —
beat 1 proof, beat 2 mechanism, beat 3 the specific objection. An interview feeds all three, but
the objection clips are the scarcest and the ones beat 3 is starved of.

### The rule that carries over from the Q&A library

**Every answer ends pointing forward to the call.** An answer that closes the loop completely
removes the reason to attend — it handles the objection, it does not deliver the consultation.

You cannot add that line to the audio, because the audio is the client's. So it goes in one of
two places: **on-screen text at the end of the clip**, or **the written answer on the page under
the video**. Write it either way; do not skip it because the client did not say it.

Each Q&A also needs **a written answer of one to two sentences for the page** — someone who reads
only that and never presses play should still have the objection handled. That sentence is yours
to write, drawn from what the client said, and it is the one place in this skill where you are
writing prose rather than cutting.

## Routing — which winner goes where

| Score | Routes to |
|---|---|
| **3/3** | Retargeting ad **and** objection answer, from the same cut. Plus the proof block |
| **2/3, failed "kills an objection"** | Retargeting ad + proof block. Not a Q&A answer |
| **2/3, failed "stands alone"** | Proof block + Q&A answer, where the page supplies context. Not an ad |
| **2/3, failed "specific proof"** | Q&A answer + proof block. It cannot lead an ad |
| **1/3** | Proof block, or nowhere |

**Some winners serve two outputs, and that is the point of the skill.** A clip that carries a
number *and* kills a named objection is one cut with two pieces of on-screen text over it — one
hook framing it as proof for the ad, one framing it as an answer for the page. Say so explicitly
in the handoff, or somebody re-cuts the same thirty seconds twice.

**What each output refuses:**

- A clip that needs the interviewer's question is **not a retargeting ad**. It can still be a
  proof-block video, where the page supplies the context.
- A clip with no number is **not a case-study breakdown**, however warm it is.
- A clip about an objection the user has never actually been given is **not a Q&A answer**. It is
  an answer to a question nobody asked.

---

## Cadence

- **Standing retargeting layer: rotate 5–10 creatives monthly.** Straight from both SOPs.
- **Pre-call library: refresh every 3–4 weeks.** Different rhythm on purpose — that audience is a
  few hundred people who each see an asset once, rather than a broad audience seeing it
  repeatedly.
- **Confirmation-page proof block: append forever.** Nothing rotates out; it only grows.
- **Collect testimonials continuously.** Every new one feeds all three.

> **Put the interview in the calendar, not on the to-do list.** One booked interview a month
> covers the retargeting refresh, the pre-call refresh and the new results on the confirmation
> page. Nobody has ever produced this content by intending to.
