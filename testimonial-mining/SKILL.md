# Testimonial Mining

**One library, three outputs.** A client interview filmed once is not one asset. It is three,
and almost everyone throws two of them away.

```
        one interview transcript
                  |
   +--------------+--------------+
   |              |              |
   1              2              3
retargeting    confirmation    objection
    ad          page proof      answer
  30–60s        results block   Q&A + pre-call
```

That is the whole reason this skill exists. The B2C SOP names testimonial videos as the **most
effective retargeting format** and says to cut them from interview footage. The confirmation
page needs a client results block stacked past the point of reason. The Q&A library needs
objections answered — and a client answering an objection beats the founder answering it every
time. **All three come out of the same hour of footage.** Cut once, place three times.

```
Step 1  intake             transcript, client, real numbers, the objections it could answer
Step 2  mark candidates    5–10 moments, timestamped
Step 3  score them         three tests — then weak moment vs weak cut
Step 4  route the winners  which of the three outputs each one feeds
Step 5  write the assets   on-screen hook, caption, ad copy
Step 6  hand off           →  09-testimonial-clips.md
```

Three hard rules that override everything below.

- **Never invent a client name, a number, a before-and-after, a timeframe or a quote.** If you
  do not have it, write `[CLIENT NAME]`, `[BEFORE FIGURE → AFTER FIGURE]`, `[TIMEFRAME]` and
  keep going. A visible placeholder is honest. A plausible invented figure is a claim the user
  runs behind paid traffic with a real person's face on it.
- **You cut. You do not write the client's lines.** Every spoken word in a clip has to be a word
  the client actually said, in the order they said it. Trimming a stumble is editing. Tidying a
  sentence into something they did not say is fabrication, and it is the one failure that turns
  a testimonial into a liability. On-screen text and captions are yours. The audio is theirs.
- **Never block on a missing answer.** Leave the bracketed placeholder, carry on, and list every
  placeholder at the end so they can fill them in one pass.

> **This skill reads text. It cannot watch the footage.** It works from a transcript, so the
> user needs one — and almost every tool they already have will produce one. Zoom and Riverside
> transcribe the recording, Loom and Descript do it on upload, and an unlisted YouTube upload
> generates captions that can be copied out. **Ask for timestamps.** A clip plan without them is
> a list of nice quotes nobody can find in the file. If all they have is a video file, the honest
> answer is: generate the transcript first, then come back — do not guess at content.

## Step 1 — Intake

Ask for all of this in one short list. This is an operator task, not a non-technical interview —
they are pasting a transcript, not being walked through a build. Accept partial answers and move.

| # | Ask | Why it changes the output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The transcript**, with timestamps if the tool gave them | the whole input |
| 2 | **Who the client is** — name, what they do, where they started | captions, the header on the proof block |
| 3 | **What they achieved** — real numbers, before → after, over how long | decides which clips can carry an ad at all |
| 4 | **The objections this footage could answer** | Step 3's first test, and output 3 entirely |
| 5 | **B2B or B2C** | routes the creative list, the copy style and the CTA |
| 6 | **What is already live** — retargeting running? confirmation page up? | decides which outputs to write now and which to park |

**Question 4 is the one that matters and the one people skip.** Without a named objection list,
scoring collapses into "which quote sounded good", and the batch comes out as five clips that all
do the same job. Ask for the objections **in the prospect's own words** — *"will this work in my
niche?"* is a clip brief; *"niche suitability"* is not.

If they say they do not have a list, they do — it is written down in four places, and
`references/interview-questions.md` says where. Their last ten call recordings, the setter's
notes, DMs and ad comments, and the people who did not buy.

**On question 3, do not accept a vague answer twice.** Ask once for the numbers. If they still do
not have them, placeholder it and tell them plainly what it costs: a clip with no number can sit
in the proof block, but it cannot carry a retargeting ad on its own.

---

### Getting the transcript

They probably already have one and do not know it. Do not let this become a blocker — it is a
two-minute step.

**Tell them to look where the interview was recorded first.** Loom, Zoom cloud recordings,
Google Meet, Riverside and Descript all produce transcripts. Whether it is switched on varies by
plan and by setting, so the instruction is *check whether yours has it*, never a promise that it
does.

**If there is none anywhere, the fallback works for everybody.** Upload the video to YouTube as
**unlisted**, wait a few minutes for automatic captions, open the video and use **Show
transcript**. Copy the lot. It costs nothing, needs no new account for anyone with a Google
login, and unlisted keeps it out of search and off their channel.

> **Treat every number in the transcript as unconfirmed until they confirm it.** Automatic
> captions reliably mangle names, figures and jargon, and this skill exists to pull *specific
> numbers* out of a client's own words. Flag every figure you lift and ask them to check it
> against the recording before it goes anywhere near an ad. A wrong number in a testimonial is
> worse than no testimonial.

**Keep the timestamps if the transcript has them.** They are what turns your output into a cut
list an editor can work from. If there are none, quote the opening few words of each clip
instead so whoever edits can find the moment.

## Step 2 — Mark the candidates

**Read the transcript, not the video.** Mark **5–10 moments**. Timestamp in, timestamp out, the
quote as spoken.

A moment is a candidate when it is one of these:

- **A specific number** — a figure, a date, a count, said out loud by the client.
- **A named before-and-after** — where they were, where they are, and how long it took.
- **A moment of doubt that resolved** — they nearly did not do it, and they say why they did.
- **The mechanism in their own words** — what actually changed in how they work. A client
  explaining the system is worth more than the founder explaining it, because they have nothing
  to gain by explaining it well.
- **An objection answered unprompted** — they raise it themselves and knock it down. These are
  the rarest and the most valuable clips in any interview.

**The five-second test:** if you can turn the moment into a headline in under five seconds, it is
a candidate. If you are still constructing the headline at ten seconds, the moment is doing too
many things at once — it is two clips or it is nothing.

Output the marking pass as a table before you score anything, so the user can see what you found
and tell you if you missed something they remember saying.

> **If the interview yields two, say two.** Cutting filler to reach a target is how a library
> fills up with assets nobody watches. An honest *"there are three clips in this, not eight"* is
> the right answer far more often than it feels like it should be. Then say what to ask next
> time — `references/interview-questions.md` exists so the next interview yields more.

Full marking method, the candidate table format and what never becomes a clip:
`references/selection.md`.

## Step 3 — Score them

Three tests. One line each, per candidate, yes or no.

1. **Does it kill one named objection?** Named — from the intake list at question 4, not one you
   imagined on the client's behalf.
2. **Does it stand alone without the rest of the interview?** Someone who has never heard of the
   offer sees only these thirty seconds. Does it make sense?
3. **Does it carry specific proof?** A number, a date, a named outcome. "It changed my life" is
   not proof. "[$X] in [TIMEFRAME]" is.

Three out of three ships first. Two out of three is usable — say which test it failed and where
that lands it in Step 4. One out of three goes to the proof block or nowhere.

### The distinction that saves the most time

Before you discard anything, decide which of these you are looking at. Getting this wrong is how
people spend an afternoon re-cutting footage that was never going to work, and bin the clip that
just needed its first two seconds moved.

> **A weak moment is unfixable — discard it and move on.**
> **A weak cut of a strong moment is fixable — re-cut it rather than binning it.**

**Weak moment.** No number, no objection, no specific. The client is agreeing rather than telling
— answering "yes, definitely" to a question that did the work for them. It could have been said
by anyone, about anything. No edit rescues this. It is not a cutting problem, it is a footage
problem, and the fix is in the next interview.

**Weak cut.** The substance is right there — the number is said, the objection is knocked down —
but the clip starts mid-sentence, runs forty seconds past its own point, or only makes sense if
you heard the interviewer's question. Every one of those is an in-point, an out-point or a line
of on-screen text. Re-cut it. `references/selection.md` § *Re-cutting* has the four moves.

## Step 4 — Route each winner to its output

Every winner goes to at least one. **Some serve two, and that is the point of the skill** — a
clip that carries a number *and* kills a named objection is a retargeting ad and a Q&A answer
from the same cut, with two different pieces of on-screen text over it.

| Output | Length | Where it lands | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Retargeting ad** | **30–60s** | the standing retargeting layer — **15–20% of total ad spend**, **5–10 creatives rotated monthly** | proof + a clean before/after, and it must stand alone |
| **2. Confirmation-page proof block** | no ceiling | the client results block on the confirmation page | anything real and verified — volume is the whole mechanism here |
| **3. Objection answer** | short | the Q&A library on the confirmation page, and beat 3 of the pre-call sequence | one named objection, answered in the client's own words |

Output 2 is the forgiving one. Stack it so they cannot scroll to the bottom — the clips that were
too slow for an ad still belong here, and in B2C the sheer volume of proof is what converts the
sceptical.

Retargeting audiences, the B2B/B2C split, the structure of each cut and the cadence for all three:
`references/the-three-outputs.md`.

## Step 5 — Write the assets

For every winner, in this order:

1. **The on-screen hook — under ten words**, readable within a second. It is the same words they
   are about to hear, not a summary of the clip. **The first frame has to stop the scroll on its
   own.** Never open mid-sentence, and never open on a random line pulled from the middle of an
   answer. A different hook on every clip in the batch — the hook is the variable being tested,
   so five clips opening the same way is one test run five times.
2. **The caption** — client name, the before, the after, the timeframe. A result with no caption
   is a picture of a number and it persuades nobody. Label verified numbers verified.
3. **For retargeting ads only, the ad copy that sits above the video.** B2B is short with arrow
   bullets. B2C is long-form, first person, story-led, and never mentions a call, a price or an
   application. Templates for both: `references/copy.md`.

Then run the self-check in `references/copy.md` § *Before you hand it over*. The non-negotiables:
every number came from the user or is a visible placeholder; no clip opens mid-sentence; no quote
has been paraphrased into something the client did not say; the CTA matches where the funnel
actually sends people.

---

## Step 6 — Hand off

Write everything into one file, **`09-testimonial-clips.md`**, in the folder they are working in.
One file, because they are going to have it open on a second screen while they scrub the timeline.

Structure it in this order:

1. **The clip list** — every winner, with timestamp in, timestamp out and the quote as spoken
2. **The scoring table** — including what you discarded and why, so nobody re-litigates it later
3. **Per clip:** which output(s) it feeds, the on-screen hook, the caption, and the ad copy where
   there is one
4. **The delivery spec** — below
5. **The placeholder list** — everything still bracketed, so they can fill it in one pass
6. **What to ask next time** — if the interview under-yielded, the two or three questions that
   would have fixed it

### The delivery spec to state every time

- **Vertical 9:16, with burned-in captions.** Most of these impressions are watched with the
  sound off, in Reels and Stories. A landscape cut of a desktop screen-share does not read on a
  phone.
- **One idea per clip.** If a cut carries two arguments, it is two clips.
- **Shoot horizontal, crop to vertical. Never the other way round.** This one is about the *next*
  interview: horizontal footage crops to 9:16 in one click, and vertical footage can never
  become a long-form breakdown or anything on a desktop. Film vertical and one-library-three-
  outputs is impossible.

### What this skill does not do

Say this plainly, in one line each:

- **It does not edit.** It writes timestamps and copy. Somebody still has to open the editor.
- **It does not caption or burn in text.** Burned-in captions are required; this skill writes the
  words that go in them, not the file.
- **It does not film or run the interview.** `references/interview-questions.md` says how to run
  one that yields clips, but the user is the one on the call.
- **It does not upload anything** to Meta, to YouTube, or to the confirmation page, and it does
  not build a campaign or set a budget.
- **It does not write ad scripts from scratch.** That is `ad-scripting`. This skill only works
  from words a client already said.

Close with the standing instruction, straight from both SOPs: **collect testimonials
continuously.** Every new interview feeds all three outputs. Rotate 5–10 retargeting creatives
monthly; refresh the pre-call library every 3–4 weeks. **Proof volume is a moat, and in B2C it is
the moat.**

## Editing a plan it already wrote

If `09-testimonial-clips.md` exists, never start over. Ask which clip is wrong and change that
entry. Adding a new interview appends a new clip block and extends the scoring table — it does
not renumber what is already cut and posted, because those numbers are in filenames on somebody's
drive by now.
