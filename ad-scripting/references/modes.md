# The two modes, and how to choose

## The selection rule

**The destination decides the mode. Not the audience, not the industry, not B2B versus B2C.**

Ask where the click is meant to land, then read across:

| Destination | Mode | CTA | Playbook |
|---|---|---|---|
| VSL page, landing page, application form, booking / calendar page, webinar registration | **Mode 2** if B2B | "click the link below" / "click learn more" | `b2b-formulas.md` |
| Same destinations, consumer offer | **B2C VSL-funnel structure** | "watch this free video" / "click below" | `b2c-angles.md` |
| The comments or DMs of the post itself | **Mode 1** | "comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send it to you" | this file |

### The B2C routing fix

Older versions of this SOP told operators the ad skill was "already built for B2C, use it
directly". Read alongside a Mode-1-only skill, that routed every B2C brief into Mode 1 — and
Mode 1's comment CTA on a VSL funnel sends paid traffic into a comment thread instead of to the
video that does the selling. The click never reaches the page. The lead event never fires.

The corrected logic:

> **A page as the destination means Mode 2 or the B2C VSL-funnel structure. Never Mode 1.**
> That holds for B2B and B2C alike.
>
> **Mode 1 exists only for audience building and DM funnels.** Being B2C is not a reason to
> use it. The only reason to use Mode 1 is that the ad is genuinely trying to start a comment
> or a DM rather than send someone to a page.

The two source SOPs agree on this once you strip the ambiguity. The B2C VSL SOP opens by saying
outright that follower/DM ads are a *different* skill, and that mixing them up is the classic
mistake — a follower-ad CTA on a VSL funnel sends traffic nowhere.

### Defaults

- Brief mentions a funnel, VSL, landing page, link, application, booking or call →
  **page-driving ad**. Pick B2B or B2C from the offer.
- Brief is explicitly about followers, DMs, comments, a DM-delivered lead magnet, audience
  building or personal-brand growth → **Mode 1**.
- Neither is clear → **ask, and wait for the answer.** Do not guess.

# Mode 1 — DM / comment ads

Selfie-style Instagram and TikTok reels that feel organic, not like ads. The ad's job is a
comment or a DM, so the whole thing lives on the post.

- **CTA:** "comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send it to you"
- **Format:** selfie-style, organic feel, casual
- **Length:** 30–45 seconds (150–200 words). 60 seconds is the absolute maximum.
- **Framework:** AIDA with open loops
- **Use when:** building audience, driving DM conversations, follower growth, content-led funnels

## Core principles

1. **Open loops constantly** — give enough information to create curiosity, never the full answer.
2. **It must not sound like an ad** — casual, like talking to a friend.
3. **Shorter is better** — 30–45 seconds ideal, 60 seconds absolute max.
4. **Specific numbers** — "$10K/month", not "good money".
5. **No AI slang** — never write "no stress, no fluff, just clarity" type filler.

## The AIDA framework

Use these as checkboxes, not rigid steps. Flow naturally between them.

### 1. Attention (hook) — first 3–5 seconds

Open a loop. Give just enough to make them curious, **not** the answer. Hook formulas are in
`hooks.md`.

### 2. Interest — 10–15 seconds

Sell the unique angle. Share the USPs, the common enemy, or the interesting approach.

- *"And I know this because..."* transitions into the personal story.
- Keep opening mini-loops throughout.

### 3. Desire — 5–10 seconds

Personal results, lifestyle, transformation. Be specific: money figures, timeframes, locations,
experiences. Paint the contrast — before → after.

*(Lifestyle is allowed here and only here. It is forbidden in B2B funnel ads — see
`mistakes.md`.)*

### 4. Action — the CTAs

**Mid-roll CTA, at roughly the 15–20 second mark.** Subtle and quick:

> "If you want [thing], drop me a follow and comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send it to you."

**Client results, straight after the first CTA.** These are for the sceptics still watching.
Always in this format:

> "[CLIENT NAME] went from [BEFORE] to [AFTER] in [TIME]"

**Final CTA:**

> "Comment [KEYWORD] and make sure you're following so I can send it."

## Keyword selection

Every ad gets its own unique, relevant keyword for tracking. Pick it from the ad's angle or
emotion. Examples: CHEF, QUIT, ESCAPE, RICH, FREEDOM, SCALE, GROWTH.

## Mode 1 output format

```
TITLE HOOK: [~8 words, on-screen text — similar to the spoken hook, but leaves out the "how"]

[Full script — casual, conversational, ready to record]
```

## Mode 1 quality checklist

- Hook opens a loop, without giving away the answer?
- First CTA within 15–20 seconds?
- Client result placeholders included?
- Unique keyword chosen?
- Under 200 words (~45 seconds)?
- Sounds like talking to a friend, not an ad?
- No AI slang or filler phrases?
- Specific numbers throughout?

# Mode 2 — B2B funnel ads

Ads that drive to a landing page, VSL or call booking. They lead with the offer, not with
curiosity.

- **CTA:** "click the link below" / "click learn more"
- **Format:** founder to camera, professional-confident
- **Length:** 45–120 seconds
- **Framework:** offer-led, with guarantee and case studies
- **Use when:** driving to a VSL page, application form, call booking page, or any external funnel

## Core principles

1. **Offer-first, always** — the guarantee or bold claim opens the ad within 5 seconds.
2. **ICP callout immediately** — name their industry or role in the first sentence.
3. **Single CTA at the end** — "click the link below" / "click learn more". No mid-roll CTA.
4. **Case studies are proof, not storytelling** — rapid-fire: name, before, after, timeframe.
5. **Professional-confident tone** — not hype-y, not casual. A founder who knows their numbers.
6. **No lifestyle content** — revenue, ROAS, CAC, clients signed. Never cars, never travel.
7. **System and installation language** — "install our system", "implement our funnel",
   "build out your account". You are selling a mechanism, not motivation.

The five formulas, their beats, their timings and a worked example for each are in
`b2b-formulas.md`.

## Mode 2 output format

```
TITLE HOOK: [~8 words, on-screen text that matches the opening line]

[Full script — professional-confident, ready to record]
```

## Mode 2 quality checklist

- CTA drives to a link click, not a comment or DM?
- ICP callout in the first 5 seconds?
- Guarantee or bold claim opens the ad?
- At least 3 case studies with specific numbers?
- Single CTA at the end, no mid-roll?
- Under 120 seconds / 400 words?
- Professional-confident tone, not hype?
- No lifestyle content — metrics only?
- Guarantee restated at the end?
- Title hook written?

# Mode swapping

A winning ad can be rewritten into the other mode as a horizontal-scaling variation — a winning
Mode 2 funnel ad becomes a Mode 1 version, or the reverse. When you do, **swap the CTA with it.**
A Mode 2 script wearing a Mode 1 CTA is exactly the routing failure this file exists to prevent.
