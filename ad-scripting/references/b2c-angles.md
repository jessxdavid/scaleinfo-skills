# B2C VSL-funnel ads — four angles

Top-of-funnel paid Meta/Instagram ads whose one job is to get a cold beginner to click and go
watch the free VSL.

## The one job

The ad does **not** sell the programme, explain the mechanism, or get the follow. It gets the
**click to the free VSL.** Everything is engineered around a single soft CTA:

> "I made a free video breaking it down — click below to watch."

The VSL does the selling. The ad just earns the click.

**The ad's job is a VSL page visit, not a booked call.** An ad that says "book a call" in B2C
kills the click-through rate.

**This is not the follower/DM ad style.** A "comment KEYWORD" CTA on a VSL funnel sends traffic
nowhere. See `modes.md` for the routing rule.

---

## The four angles

Mix all four across every batch. 8–10 top-of-funnel scripts.

### 1. Story-led / problem callout — the workhorse

Founder to camera. Opens on the viewer's exact pain, empathises, agrees it is not their fault,
teases the unique mechanism, and points to the free video. This is the highest-volume angle —
most of the batch.

### 2. Live proof / demonstration

The founder narrating a real result in real time. Radical transparency as the hook.

*Worked reference — the worked example: screen-recorded live trades,* "I just made $31,200 across 26
accounts, I'm done for the week."

### 3. Student testimonial

A real student telling their before → after to camera.

*Worked reference — the worked example:* "I've taken over $50K in payouts in two months... I was
unprofitable for two years before this."

### 4. Founder origin / authority

The compressed rags-to-results story that makes the outcome feel achievable for a beginner.

> Angles 2 and 3 — live proof and student testimonial — double as the retargeting workhorses.
> Script them so they work in both places.

**Never reuse the the worked example figures.** They belong to that brand. They are here to show the
shape of a specific claim, not to be borrowed. Use the user's own, or a visible placeholder.

## Video ad structure — story-led angle

Keep it feeling organic, like a founder sharing rather than an ad. **30–90 seconds.**

1. **Hook — first 3 seconds.** Call out the pain, or drop a contrarian claim.
   *"The reason you're not profitable isn't what you think."* /
   *"While you're swiping, guys like this are getting approached."*
2. **Empathy / relatability.** Name their exact cycle and take the blame off them.
   *"You're stuck blowing funded accounts, taking trades you know you shouldn't... and I've been there."*
3. **Tease the mechanism.** Hint at the unique "how" without teaching it.
   *"Looking back, I could've shaved a year off just by having a model with a low RR and high win rate."*
4. **Soft CTA to the free video.**
   *"I made a completely free video breaking down my whole journey — click below to check it out."*

**Do not resolve the open loop.** The ad's only job is curiosity → click. If they get the answer
in the ad, they never click. The VSL delivers the answer.

## Length and tone

- **Video:** 30–90 seconds. Fast cuts, real settings, founder-to-camera or screen-share proof.
  Organic feel, never studio-polished-ad feel.
- **Copy:** long-form story, first person, warm, "rooting for you" energy.
- **Tone:** aspirational and empathetic — meet them in the pain, then open the door to the free
  video.
- **One idea per ad.** If it needs a second breath to explain, it is two ads.

## Ad copy — the text below the video

The winning B2C pattern is **long-form, first-person, story-led** copy that reads like a personal
note, not a punchy direct-response block.

Real the worked example example:

```
Trading doesn't need to be hard, and it doesn't even need to be complicated.

The reason why you aren't consistently profitable isn't what you think...

You're stuck in this cycle of blowing funded accounts, taking trades you know you
shouldn't have, and feeling glued to the charts like it's another 9-5.

And look, I've been there. It took me a year and a half to turn profitable.

Looking back, I could have shaved off more than a year simply by having:
1) a consistent model with a low RR and high win rate,
2) a mentor with a real track record,
3) a community to hold me accountable.

I just made a completely free video where I broke down my entire journey from blown
accounts to 6 figures in monthly profits.

Click below to check it out.

Rooting for you,
- [Name]
```

The shape to reproduce, in order:

1. A disarming opening line that contradicts what they expect.
2. The open loop — *"isn't what you think..."*
3. Their exact cycle, in their own words.
4. *"And look, I've been there"* — plus how long it actually took.
5. The numbered list of what would have short-cut it. This is the mechanism tease.
6. The free video.
7. A warm sign-off with a first name.

**Note what it never says:** it never mentions a call, a price, or an application.

The CTA in the copy points at the VSL page with a **Learn More** button — never "comment",
never "DM".

## B2C retargeting

5 scripts per batch.

**Audiences:** VSL page visitors who did not submit the form, VSL viewers (50%+) who did not
book, and 90-day engagers. **Budget:** 15–20% of total ad spend.

Creative types, most effective first:

1. **Testimonial videos** — cut from interview footage.
2. **Live proof / transparency clips** — real wins and losses in real time.
3. **Case study breakdowns** — one student's journey with specific numbers.
4. **Objection-handling ads** — *"Tried courses before? Here's why this is different."*
5. **Lifestyle / identity** — the freedom the outcome buys, tied back to the mechanism.

Rotate **5–10 retargeting creatives monthly** and collect new testimonials constantly.
**Proof volume is a B2C moat.**

> Lifestyle content is permitted in B2C retargeting *only when it is tied back to the mechanism*.
> It remains forbidden in every B2B ad — see `mistakes.md`.

---

## Where these ads sit

These are Step 2 (top-of-funnel ads) of the B2C call funnel. They send cold traffic to the VSL
landing page. **Script them after the offer/messaging and the VSL angle are locked**, so the ad's
hook and the VSL's opening promise line up. A hook that promises something the VSL does not open
with is the most common reason a B2C funnel leaks at the page.
