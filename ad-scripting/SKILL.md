# Ad Scripting

Scripts a full batch of video ads plus the ad copy that sits above each one.

```
Step 1  pick the mode          ← the highest-value decision in this skill
Step 2  gather the intake
Step 3  script the batch       →  02-tof-ads.md
Step 4  write the ad copy
Step 5  self-check
Step 6  hand off               →  08-retargeting-ads.md
```

Two hard rules that override everything below:

- **Never invent a client name, a revenue figure, a timeframe or a result.** If you do not
  have it, write `[CASE STUDY 1 — NAME, BEFORE, AFTER, TIMEFRAME]` and keep going. A visible
  placeholder is honest. A plausible invented `$40K/mo` is a lie the user will read on camera.
- **Never block on a missing answer.** Leave a `[bracketed placeholder]`, carry on, and list
  every placeholder at the end so the user can fill them in one pass.

## Step 1 — Pick the mode

Getting this wrong is the classic failure. The two modes have two different calls to action,
and the wrong CTA sends the traffic nowhere.

### The rule: the destination decides the mode

Ask one question only — **where does the click go?**

| Where the traffic is meant to land | Mode | CTA |
|---|---|---|
| VSL page, landing page, application form, booking / calendar page, webinar registration, any external page | **Mode 2** (B2B) or the **B2C VSL-funnel structure** (B2C) | "click the link below" / "watch this free video" |
| The comments or DMs of the post itself — lead magnet by DM, follower growth, audience building, content-led funnel | **Mode 1** | "comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send it to you" |

**B2B versus B2C does not decide the mode. The destination does.**

Once the destination says "a page", the audience decides only *which* page-driving playbook:

- B2B / agency / coaching / done-for-you → **Mode 2**, `references/b2b-formulas.md`
- B2C / consumer / biz-opp / VSL funnel → **B2C angles**, `references/b2c-angles.md`

### The B2C routing fix — read this before defaulting anything

Earlier versions of this SOP said the ad skill was "already built for B2C, use it directly",
which pointed B2C at Mode 1. **That is wrong whenever there is a page to click to.** Mode 1's
"comment KEYWORD" CTA on a VSL funnel sends paid traffic into a comment thread instead of the
video that does the selling.

So, stated unambiguously:

> **If the user is driving to a VSL page, a landing page, an application form or a booking
> page, it is Mode 2 or the B2C VSL-funnel structure — never Mode 1 — regardless of whether
> the offer is B2B or B2C.**
>
> **Mode 1 is only ever for audience building and DM funnels.** Being B2C is not a reason to
> use Mode 1. A comment/DM destination is the only reason to use Mode 1.

### Defaults, and when to ask

Default to a **page-driving ad** if the brief mentions a funnel, a VSL, a landing page, a
link, an application, a booking or a call. Then pick B2B or B2C from the offer.

Default to **Mode 1** only if the brief is explicitly about followers, DMs, comments, a lead
magnet sent by DM, audience building or personal-brand growth.

If neither is clear, **ask, and wait**:

> **Where do you want the click to go?**
>
> **1. To a page** — my VSL, landing page, application form or booking calendar.
> **2. To my comments or DMs** — I'll send them something and grow the audience.
>
> Reply **1** or **2**.

Full detail on both modes, including Mode 1's AIDA structure: `references/modes.md`.

## Step 2 — Gather the intake

**Output quality tracks input specificity.** An ad written from a one-line brief reads like a
one-line brief. Ask for these four before scripting anything:

1. **The offer, in full** — the result, the timeframe, the guarantee, the risk reversal.
2. **The ICP, specifically.** Not "business owners". *"Ecommerce brands doing $1M+/yr on Shopify."*
3. **Three to five case studies** — name, before, after, how long.
4. **Competitor ads to model** — transcripts pasted in, or Meta Ad Library links. If a brand
   has run the same ad for months, it works; that is the whole signal.

Ask for all four in one short list, not one question at a time — this is an operator task, not
a non-technical interview. Accept partial answers and move on.

Then work in this order, which produces better ads than handing over the whole job at once:
**core hook concept → structure outlined step by step → scripted in proper format.**

If a case study comes back vague ("a client did really well"), ask once for the numbers. If
they still do not have them, placeholder it. Do not round, estimate, or fill in a likely figure.

## Step 3 — Script the batch

| | Top of funnel | Retargeting | Mix across the batch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B2B** | 5–10 scripts | 3–5 scripts | all five formulas represented |
| **B2C** | 8–10 scripts | 5 scripts | all four angles represented |

Never run a whole batch on one formula or one angle. Include at least one ultra-short ad
(20–30s) in every B2B batch.

Every script comes out in this shape:

```
TITLE HOOK: [~8 words of on-screen text, matching the opening spoken line]

[Full script — ready to record]
```

The title hook is roughly **8 words** and is **the same words they are about to hear**, not a
summary of the ad. It leaves out the "how". **A different hook on every single ad in the
batch** — the hook is the variable being tested, so ten ads opening the same way is one test
run ten times. Formulas and rules: `references/hooks.md`.

In Mode 1, every ad also gets its own unique tracking keyword.

- B2B beats, timings and worked examples: `references/b2b-formulas.md`
- B2C angles and the 30–90s structure: `references/b2c-angles.md`
- Retargeting creative types and audiences: `references/b2c-angles.md` § Retargeting

## Step 4 — Write the ad copy

This is the post copy sitting above the video, not the script. It is a separate deliverable and
every ad gets one.

**B2B — short, arrow bullets:**

```
[Guarantee headline]

Here's what we'll do:
→ [Benefit 1]
→ [Benefit 2]
→ [Benefit 3]

[Case study] went from X to Y in Z days.
[Case study] went from X to Y in Z days.

Click below to see how.
```

**B2C — long-form, first person, story-led.** It should read like a personal note, not a punchy
direct-response block: the pain, the "I've been there", the three things that would have short-cut
it, the free video, a warm sign-off. It never mentions a call, a price or an application. The
worked example and the full style notes are in `references/b2c-angles.md`.

The B2C copy CTA points at the VSL page with a **Learn More** button — never "comment", never "DM".

## Step 5 — Self-check

Read `references/mistakes.md` and check the batch against it before handing anything over. The
non-negotiables:

- Every CTA matches the mode. No "comment KEYWORD" on a funnel ad.
- A different hook on every ad.
- No hook that closes its own loop.
- No lifestyle content in any B2B ad — revenue, ROAS, CAC, clients signed.
- Every number is specific and came from the user, or is a visible placeholder.

**The read-it-aloud test.** Read every script out loud. If you would not say it in a
conversation, cut it. This is how AI slang gets caught: *"No stress. No fluff. Just results."*
*"...no fluff, just clarity."* Any tricolon of clipped negations is an instant credibility
killer and must not survive to the final draft.

Then list every `[bracketed placeholder]` still in the batch, so the user knows exactly what
they owe before filming.

## Step 6 — Hand off

Write two files into the user's working directory:

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `02-tof-ads.md` | the top-of-funnel batch — every script with its title hook, plus its ad copy block |
| `08-retargeting-ads.md` | the retargeting batch, same shape |

Tell them, in one line each, what this skill does **not** do:

- It does not **film** anything. A phone is fine, one take per script, real settings beat a studio.
- It does not **edit** or produce the video, and does not cut it to 9:16.
- It does not **caption**. Burned-in captions are required — most of the audience watches muted.
- It does not **upload to Meta**, build the campaign, set budgets or targeting, or manage the
  ad account.
- It does not **run or scale campaigns**. Budgets, kill rules and scaling rates live in the
  media-buying playbooks, not here.

Close with the standing instruction: **creative is consumable — add 3–5 new ads every 2 weeks.**
Scripting a batch is not a one-time job. When one wins, scale it horizontally
(`references/hooks.md` § Horizontal scaling).
