# Webinar registration VSL

The video whose only job is to get someone to register for an event. The webinar sells the offer;
this video sells the seat.

**3–5 minutes · ~450–750 words.**

---

## The structure, and what gets cut

Ten sections exist in this framework. A webinar VSL uses seven of them and compresses or skips
three, because the event does that work:

```
1.  Pattern interrupt / bold claim        keep
2.  Authority / origin story              keep, 30–60s
3.  Vehicle / opportunity reveal          keep
4.  USP proof                             keep
5.  Social proof / student results        keep
6.  Urgency / window closing              keep
7.  Offer / event preview                 keep — but it is an EVENT preview, not an offer stack
8.  Objection handling / mindset reframe   compress or skip — the webinar does this
9.  Emotional close                       keep
10. CTA + scarcity                        keep
```

The running order for a webinar VSL, stated the way the source document states it:

> Bold Claim → Authority → Vehicle → USP Proof → Social Proof → Urgency → Event Preview →
> Emotional Close → CTA

Use the framework as a flexible backbone. Not every section appears in every VSL — adapt to the
offer, the audience and the type.

---

## The ten sections

### 1. Pattern interrupt / bold claim

Open with a specific, attention-grabbing result or statement. No warm-up, no introduction. Drop
the viewer into something they cannot ignore. Specific numbers and timeframes.

Unlike the B2B qualification gate, this beat is not a filter. It is a stop.

### 2. Authority / origin story

Establish credibility fast — **compress into 30–60 seconds**. Include: age if young, specific
results, lifestyle proof, press or features. Not a biography; a relevant flex that earns the
right to teach.

At 3–5 minutes total, 60 seconds of authority is already a fifth of the video. Hold the line.

### 3. Vehicle / opportunity reveal

Name the business model, skill or method. Explain *what* it is just enough to create
understanding, not enough to execute independently. Frame it as uniquely advantageous **right
now**.

For novel concepts, use an analogy — *connector economy = the Uber / Airbnb model*. An analogy
does in one sentence what an explanation does in five, which matters at this length.

### 4. USP proof

Convey why this specific opportunity makes logical sense. Takes different forms depending on the
offer:

- **Income / business offers:** a **math breakdown**. Break the income claim into daily or weekly
  numbers so it feels achievable.
  *"$412/day × 6 days = $2,472/week"* · *"5 calls → 4 show → 1 close = $250–$500/day"*
- **Non-financial offers** (dating, health, and so on): a different proof mechanism — unique
  method advantage, a contrarian insight, "why this works when everything else doesn't" framing,
  or a compelling analogy.
- **The goal either way:** make the viewer think *"okay, that actually makes sense"*.

The maths must be their numbers. If you do not have the income claim, `[INCOME CLAIM]` and
`[DAILY BREAKDOWN]` — a made-up arithmetic chain is the most damaging thing you can put in a VSL,
because it looks the most verifiable.

### 5. Social proof / student results

**2–4 specific named results.** Always the same pattern:

> "[Name] went from [before] to [after] in [timeframe]."

Include variety — different backgrounds, different speeds of results, different life changes. Use
`[NAME]` and `[RESULT SUMMARY]` placeholders if real names are not provided yet.

### 6. Urgency / window closing

Why **now**, specifically. Options: market timing, competition rising, limited spots, seasonal
demand, the method getting saturated, a specific event date approaching.

**Must feel real, not manufactured.** For a webinar there is usually a genuine one sitting right
there: the event has a date. Use it before you reach for anything softer.

### 7. Offer / event preview

- **Webinar:** 3–5 bullet points of what they will learn on the event, plus bonuses for showing
  up live. Keep it brief — you are selling the seat, not the content.
- **Cold traffic:** the full offer stack — what is included, pricing, guarantee, risk reversal.
  Layer value before revealing price.

Selling the content instead of the seat is the classic failure here. Every bullet should raise a
question the event answers, not answer it.

### 8. Objection handling / mindset reframe

Address the top 1–2 objections the viewer is thinking: *"I've seen this before"*, *"I've tried and
failed"*, *"sounds too good to be true"*. Reframe the difference between this and what they have
tried.

Primarily for cold-traffic VSLs. **Webinar VSLs skip or compress this** — registration is a low
commitment, and objection handling at this stage spends time arguing them out of something they
have not been asked to buy yet.

### 9. Emotional close

Get the viewer over the line psychologically. Several techniques work — pick what fits the
script's energy:

- **Two versions of you** — paint the inaction scenario against the action scenario, 30 days out
- **Fear of the same year repeating** — *"Don't let 2026 look like 2025"*
- **Identity shift** — *"The people who win aren't smarter, they just take action"*
- **Opportunity cost** — what staying stuck actually costs them over 12 months

The goal is to make inaction feel more painful than action.

### 10. CTA + scarcity

Specific, clear instructions: click below, enter name / email / phone **correctly**, limited
spots. Reinforce scarcity one final time. End with a forward-looking statement —
*"I'll see you on the other side."*

"Correctly" is not padding: a mistyped phone number on a registration form is a lead that no
reminder sequence can reach.

## Section timing at a glance

For a 4-minute cut (~600 words).

```
1.  Pattern interrupt / bold claim   0:00–0:15
2.  Authority                        0:15–1:00   (30–60s, hard cap)
3.  Vehicle / opportunity reveal     1:00–1:35
4.  USP proof                        1:35–2:10
5.  Social proof, 2–4 results        2:10–2:45
6.  Urgency / window closing         2:45–3:00
7.  Event preview, 3–5 bullets       3:00–3:25
9.  Emotional close                  3:25–3:45
10. CTA + scarcity                   3:45–4:00
```

Section 8 is absent by design.

## The cold-traffic setting — a fallback, not a fourth route

The same ten sections run at **5–10 minutes (~750–1,500 words)** for cold traffic: go deeper on
the offer stack, objection handling and risk reversal, and often add a second CTA midway.

Most of the time you should not be here. A cold-traffic B2B offer belongs in Format A; a
cold-traffic B2C biz-opp offer belongs in the 11 beats. Both were written for exactly that job and
both are more specific than this.

Reach for the cold-traffic setting only when neither fits — for example a consumer offer that
sells directly from the video with no call and no webinar. When you do, **say which framework you
are using and why**, so the user is not left assuming they got the B2C framework their SOP
specifies.

There is also a rare **extended** band in this framework: 10–20 minutes (~1,500–3,000 words), for
selling a high-ticket offer directly from the VSL. It is genuinely rare. Do not propose it unless
the user asks for a direct-purchase VSL with no call step at all.

## Notes on the source

This framework came from a document whose own header was malformed — the skill name and
description had collapsed into a single Markdown heading with no YAML fences at all. Two of its
sections pointed at a companion file of three annotated example scripts that has never existed —
not in the Drive, not anywhere else. Both dead pointers are dropped here; the worked examples
above are the ones the source document actually contained.
