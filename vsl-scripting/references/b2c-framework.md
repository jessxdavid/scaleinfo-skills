# B2C — the 11-beat framework

For info products, courses, certifications, coaching and biz-opp offers sold to consumers.

**8–15 minutes · ~1,200–2,250 words · 11 beats, in order.**

This framework is reverse-engineered from the worked example's 9-minute VSL and is specified by the B2C
VSL Call Funnel SOP. It is the framework the SOP means when it says "written with the
`vsl-scripting` skill, following the Step 3 framework exactly".

---

## Two things to hold on to before you write a word

**The VSL is not optional here — it is the centre of the funnel.** Unlike B2B, the offer is sold
*inside* the VSL, and the whole landing page is built around it. Everything else on that page
exists to get the video watched.

**8–15 minutes, and that length is deliberate.** B2C biz-opp VSLs run longer than B2B because
beginners need more belief-building. The origin story and the proof of repeatability are what make
an ordinary viewer think *that could be me*, and they cannot be rushed. If a draft comes in at
six minutes, something load-bearing has been skipped.

It embeds on the landing page **above the form**, auto-play muted with captions. That is the
funnel builder's job, not this skill's — but it is why the first thirty seconds have to work
silently, on captions alone.

---

## What the beats are built out of

Three ingredients from the offer, all of which should already exist before scripting. If they do
not, the video will be structurally weak no matter how well it is written — say so rather than
writing around it.

1. **The unique mechanism.** The named "how". Contrarian — the opposite of what everyone else
   teaches. It belongs in the headline as well as the video.
   *Worked example: "psychology first, not strategy", plus a high win rate (~70%) at a low risk-to-reward.*
2. **The origin / identity story.** A relatable "I was exactly where you are" founder story:
   rags-to-results, real numbers, a real low point. The story is the proof that an ordinary
   beginner can do it.
   *the founder: a fast-food job at $500/month → failed dropshipping → failed SMMA → lost on options →
   fixed his psychology → $100K by 18.*
3. **A specific dream-outcome promise with objections pre-handled.**
   Formula: **"How to [dream outcome] without [thing they hate], even if [biggest objection]."**
   *Worked example: "Discover the stupid simple strategy turning even beginners profitable… first payout in
   12 weeks, even if you've failed before, work a full-time job, or aren't naturally
   disciplined."*

The mechanism is built as the **reversal of the enemy** — whatever everyone else does that this
founder is against: chasing big wins, complicated indicators, fake gurus. If the user has not
named their enemy, ask. It is one question and it sharpens beats 2, 6 and 8 at once.

## The 11 beats

### 1. Callout + qualify — 0:00–0:30

Speak to their exact situation and pain.

> "You've watched hundreds of hours on YouTube, you know what a liquidity sweep is, but you're
> still inconsistent… sound familiar?"

This is a callout, not a B2B qualification gate. The viewer should feel **seen**, not screened.
The qualifying happens on the form later; here it happens by recognition — the right person
thinks *that's me*, the wrong person leaves on their own.

Runs muted with captions on auto-play, so it has to land as text.

### 2. Reframe the problem, seed the mechanism

Do not say they need a better strategy. Say they are missing the psychological or foundational
piece.

> "You're not missing a better strategy. You're missing the psychological foundation."

Seed only. Name the shape of the answer, do not explain it — that is beat 6. The gap between
beats 2 and 6 is the open loop that keeps a beginner watching for nine minutes.

### 3. Origin story

Relatable rags-to-results, with **real numbers and a real low point**: zero → rock bottom →
breakthrough → success.

The low point is not optional and it is not decoration. It is what makes the viewer believe the
distance is crossable. A story that skips straight from zero to success proves the founder is
special, which is the opposite of what this beat is for.

Real numbers throughout. If the user has not given them, `[LOW POINT — WHAT HAPPENED, WHEN]`,
`[FIRST RESULT — AMOUNT, TIMEFRAME]`. Never invent a rock bottom.

`[SHOW LIFESTYLE B-ROLL]` and `[SHOW EARLY SCREENSHOTS]` belong on this route — lifestyle proof
is a primary credibility mechanism in B2C, unlike B2B where it is banned.

### 4. The discovery / proof of repeatability

Show how you proved the system works on **other ordinary people**.

> "I taught the first student, then a second student got his first payout, that's when we knew we had a system."

Include the **free community → paid programme evolution**: the founder helped people for free,
it kept working, and the programme is what that turned into. It answers "why are you charging for
this" before it is asked.

This beat is what separates "he's talented" from "it's a system". Do not merge it into beat 5 —
it does a different job. Beat 4 proves repeatability. Beat 5 proves volume.

### 5. Proof stack

Reviews, on-screen payouts, testimonial clips woven through, plus the **transparency play**.

> the worked example: "every trade, win or loss, is on YouTube in real time."

2–4 named results, each as *"[Name] went from [before] to [after] in [timeframe]"*. Vary them —
different backgrounds, different speeds, different life changes — so the viewer can find
themselves in one.

`[SHOW PAYOUT SCREENSHOTS]` · `[CUT TO TESTIMONIAL CLIP]` · `[SHOW REVIEW COUNT]`

The transparency / anti-guru trust play is its own move and belongs here. Radical openness about
losses buys more credibility with a burned beginner than another win screenshot does.

### 6. Why it's different: the mechanism, explained

Spell it out, and explain why it beats the enemy approach.

> "High win rate fixes your psychology… we stack small wins, boringly and reliably."

This is the payoff of the loop opened in beat 2. Explain it well enough that the viewer
understands *why* it works, and not so well that they think they can go and do it alone. The
mechanism needs a name — if the user has not named it, `[MECHANISM NAME]` and flag it.

### 7. The offer stack

Walk through every component **one at a time**. Not a list read quickly — each component named,
then what it does for them.

the worked example's stack, as an illustration of the depth expected: onboarding call, personalised plan,
accountability group, trade reviews, daily live calls, priority access, 24/7 coach, AI tool,
weekly sessions, course.

The underlying shape of a B2C offer is **accountability + education + access + proof**, and the
accountability components are the ones to dwell on — they are the answer to beat 8.

Typical price is $2K–$7K, and **financing is not optional**: most beginners cannot pay in full.
Removing financing friction is often the single biggest lever on close rate. Whether the price
appears in the video at all is the user's call — ask, and if it does not, the CTA still has to
make clear that this is a paid programme.

### 8. Pre-empt the big objection

> "You're thinking you've tried courses before, here's the difference: accountability, not more
> videos."

There is one big objection in B2C and it is almost always this. Name it in their words before
they finish thinking it, and answer with the mechanism difference, not with more features.

### 9. CTA to apply

Send them to the form or the booking. Mechanical and clear: click below the video, fill in the
form, what happens next.

This is the first of two CTAs. Do not put the risk reversal before it — leading with a guarantee
is the mistake this framework is built to avoid.

### 10. Conditional risk reversal

The secondary guarantee, **with conditions**, backed by a believable stat.

> the worked example: "First payout in 12 weeks or we work for free until you do — as long as you watch every
> module, join 50% of calls, and apply the concepts."
> Backed by: "~1 in 2 committed students get a payout in their first 12 weeks."

**Do not lead with the guarantee.** Lead with the unique mechanism and the story. The risk
reversal is a closing device near the end: conditional and believable. The guarantee supports the
offer — it is not the offer.

The conditions are what make it believable, so keep them in. A guarantee with no conditions reads
as either a lie or desperation.

Never invent a guarantee. If the user has not offered one, skip the beat and say you skipped it.

### 11. Final CTA + identity challenge

> "Are you ready to stop gambling and actually start trading? It takes action on your end."

Second CTA, and it closes on identity rather than mechanics. The viewer is being asked to decide
who they are, not just what to click. Put the scarcity close here — the real date, the real cohort
cap, the real market timing.

## Beat timing at a glance

For an 11-minute cut (~1,650 words). Beats 3, 5, 6 and 7 carry the length.

```
1.  Callout + qualify                    0:00–0:30
2.  Reframe + seed the mechanism         0:30–1:15
3.  Origin story                         1:15–3:00
4.  Discovery / proof of repeatability   3:00–4:15
5.  Proof stack                          4:15–5:45
6.  Why it's different — the mechanism   5:45–7:15
7.  The offer stack                      7:15–9:15
8.  Pre-empt the big objection           9:15–9:50
9.  CTA to apply                         9:50–10:15
10. Conditional risk reversal            10:15–10:45
11. Final CTA + identity challenge       10:45–11:05
```

## What the page around it needs from you

Not this skill's job to build, but the script has to match it, so check the copy lines up:

- The **headline** on the page is the unique angle / mechanism, and it must match the ad.
- The **sub-headline** pre-handles the biggest objection —
  *"Even if you've failed before, work a full-time job, or aren't naturally disciplined."*
- The **form** asks for name, email, phone plus one financial qualification question —
  *"Do you have capital to invest in yourself right now?"*

If beat 1 of the script and the page headline are saying different things, one of them is wrong.
Flag it rather than quietly writing a third version.
