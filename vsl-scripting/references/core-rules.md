# Core rules, tone and stage directions

The merged rule set. Rules 1–7 apply to every route. Rules 8–14 are route-scoped and several of
them invert between B2B and B2C — check the route before applying one.

---

## Universal rules

### 1. Specific numbers, always

"$10K/month", not "good money". "29 days", not "quickly". "8x ROAS", not "great returns".

A vague number is worse than no number: it reads as something being hidden. If you do not have
the figure, a visible `[REVENUE FIGURE]` is stronger than a hedge.

### 2. First person, conversational

Like a voice note to a friend, not a script. Contractions. Short sentences. Sentence fragments
where a person would use one.

Read it out loud in your head. If you would not say it, do not write it — the founder has to say
every word of this on camera.

### 3. NO AI jargon

Never write these. They are banned outright, in every route, including where they would otherwise
pass the filler test:

- **"no stress, just execution"**
- **"no fluff, just clarity"**
- **"here's the thing though"**

**The pattern to spot:** the first two share a shape — *"no X, just Y"* / *"not X, but Y"* — a
construction that sounds like a slogan and carries no information. Anything of that shape gets
deleted and the sentence rewritten to say something. Every sentence must move the script forward.

### 4. Open loops

Create curiosity throughout, especially in the first 30 seconds. Give enough to hook, never the
full answer early.

The clearest worked example is the B2C route: beat 2 names the shape of the mechanism, beat 6
explains it, and the four beats in between are held open by that gap. Build the same tension into
whatever route you are on.

### 5. Match client tonality

Pull voice and energy from the client's existing content in the project. The script should sound
like **them**, not like a copywriter. If you have their content, mirror sentence length, the words
they repeat, how formal they are, whether they swear.

If you have no samples, say so and write to the route's default band (below), rather than
inventing a personality for them.

### 6. Stage directions in brackets

`[SHOW STRIPE DASHBOARD]` · `[SHOW CLIENT RESULTS]` · `[SHOW LIFESTYLE B-ROLL]` — wherever visual
proof should appear. See the fuller list below.

### 7. No filler language

Cut "here's the thing", "now listen", "and honestly", "now", "so", "and here's the thing" —
unless they genuinely add energy at a specific moment.

The test is per instance, not per word. One "here's the thing" landing on the single most
important reframe in the video earns its place. Six of them are a tic. Note that "here's the thing
**though**" never passes, whatever it is doing — rule 3 outranks this one.

## Route-scoped rules

### 8. Qualify first, sell second  ·  B2B

Every B2B VSL should filter viewers within the first 10 seconds. The opening states who this is
for before it states what it does.

On the B2C route the opening does the opposite job: it makes the viewer feel recognised, and the
form does the qualifying later.

### 9. Metrics over lifestyle  ·  B2B

Revenue, ROAS, CAC, LTV, growth multiples. **Never mention your car or your penthouse.** No age
flex. No "how I got started".

### 9b. Lifestyle proof is allowed and expected  ·  B2C

The inversion, and it is deliberate. In B2C, lifestyle proof is a primary credibility mechanism
and the origin story is a load-bearing beat. `[SHOW LIFESTYLE B-ROLL]` is a legitimate direction
on this route and a defect on the other one.

The two routes are not disagreeing about taste. They are selling to different people: an operator
buys a return, a beginner buys a life they can picture.

### 10. Specialisation is the differentiator  ·  B2B

"We only do X" is more powerful than "we do everything". Narrow beats broad every time with a
business buyer.

### 11. Process over promises  ·  B2B

Describe the system, not just the outcome. B2B buyers want to understand **how**.

The B2C equivalent is beat 6 — the mechanism explained, and explained specifically enough that
the viewer understands why it beats the enemy approach.

### 12. Disqualify actively  ·  B2B

Saying who you **don't** work with builds more trust than saying who you do. It is a required
section, not an optional one.

### 13. Application framing  ·  B2B

*"If we're not a fit, I'll tell you"* converts higher than "sign up now". Drive to an
application, not a purchase.

### 14. Scarcity

- **B2B — no manufactured scarcity.** *"Limited capacity because we don't take on more clients
  than we can serve"* beats *"only 5 spots left"*. If there is no real constraint, there is no
  scarcity line.
- **B2C and webinar — a scarcity close is required.** It is beat 11 on the B2C route and section
  10 on the webinar route. It still has to be real: a genuine date, a genuine cohort cap, genuine
  market timing, genuine saturation. Manufactured urgency fails on both routes; the difference is
  only whether the beat is mandatory.

Never invent the constraint. If the user has no real one, ask what it is — most offers have a
cohort start, a call capacity or a price change that nobody thought to mention.

### 15. The guarantee

- **B2B** — no hard guarantee; too many variables. A strong confidence statement instead:
  *"I've never had to refund a client because of poor performance."*
- **B2C** — a conditional risk reversal near the end, backed by a believable stat, with its
  conditions stated. **Never lead with it.** The guarantee supports the offer, it is not the
  offer.

## Tone calibration

VSLs sit on a spectrum from boardroom to founder-bro. Calibrate to the industry and the founder's
natural voice.

**B2B bands:**

| Band | Fits | Example brands |
|---|---|---|
| **Professional-authoritative** | SaaS, enterprise services, established agencies | Kraken Digital |
| **Professional-casual** | Growth agencies, marketing services, DFY offers | Scale Info, Viral Coach |
| **Founder-authentic** | Newer agencies, personal brand-led services | The Cultivator |

**Default to professional-casual** unless the client's existing content clearly sits elsewhere.
Pull tonality from their content and match it.

**B2C:** warm, first-person, relatable — a founder who was recently where the viewer is now, not
an authority talking down. B2B sells ROI to operators; B2C sells the dream, the identity, and the
feeling of finally finding the answer. A beginner buys when they believe (a) they can actually do
it, (b) it is coming from someone they trust, and (c) the mechanism feels different from
everything that failed them before.

**Never hype.** Confident and direct on B2B; warm and specific on B2C. Neither route is served by
sounding desperate or over-the-top excited.

## Stage directions

Bracketed, capitalised, on their own line or inline before the claim they support. Every results
claim gets one. A number with no visual is a claim; a number with a screenshot is proof.

**Proof:**
`[SHOW STRIPE DASHBOARD]` · `[SHOW CLIENT RESULTS]` · `[SHOW RESULTS GRAPH]` ·
`[SHOW ACCOUNT STRUCTURE]` · `[SHOW DASHBOARD]` · `[SHOW PAYOUT SCREENSHOTS]` ·
`[SHOW REVIEW COUNT]` · `[SHOW BEFORE/AFTER]`

**Footage:**
`[CUT TO TESTIMONIAL CLIP]` · `[CUT TO CLIENT TESTIMONIAL CLIP — 30–60s]` ·
`[SHOW LIFESTYLE B-ROLL]` *(B2C only)*

**Structure, for the person filming:**
`[SECTION 4 — PROOF STACK · 1:40]` at each section boundary, so the founder can film in takes and
the editor can find the cut.

Direct what the camera does, never what the client says. If a testimonial clip does not exist yet,
mark it as needed rather than writing words for a real person to have said.

## The self-check, in order

Run this over the finished draft before showing anyone. Fix, do not report.

1. Every number, name, client and result traces back to something the user told you. Anything
   else becomes a `[PLACEHOLDER]`.
2. No banned phrase and no *"no X, just Y"* construction survives.
3. No vague quantity where a specific one belongs.
4. Filler cut, per instance.
5. Proof type matches the route — lifestyle out of B2B, lifestyle and origin story present in B2C.
6. Scarcity matches the route — absent or capacity-based in B2B, present and real in B2C/webinar.
7. B2B only: the first ten seconds qualify, and the disqualification section exists.
8. Every results claim carries a stage direction.
9. Word count sits inside the route's band.
10. Read it aloud. Anything the founder would stumble over gets rewritten.
