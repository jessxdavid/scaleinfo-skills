# B2B Format A — Direct Authority VSL

The founder speaks directly to camera, walks through the system, shows results, and drives to an
application. Best for cold-traffic landing pages.

**5–10 minutes · ~750–1,500 words · 10 sections.**

Every section exists for a reason. Adapt length and emphasis to the offer; do not drop sections
without saying why.

Reference examples of this format in the wild, from the SOP: Kraken Digital
(`go.kraken-digital.com/100m-google-ads-growth`), The Cultivator (`thecultivator.co/pagewithvideos`).

---

## 1. Qualification gate + bold claim

Open by immediately qualifying the viewer **and** stating a specific outcome. This is not a
pattern interrupt for entertainment — it is a filter.

**Formula:**

> "If you're a [business type] doing [revenue/spend threshold], here's what we'll do for you:
> [specific outcome] in [timeframe]."

**Why it works:** B2B viewers are busy. They need to know in five seconds whether this is for
them. Qualification-first framing also increases perceived exclusivity.

**Example:**

> "If you're a business making over $1 million a year and you aren't already profitably scaling
> on Google Ads, here's what we'll do for you."

If the threshold number is not supplied, write `[REVENUE THRESHOLD]` — do not pick one.

---

## 2. Problem identification — two scenarios

Paint exactly **two** scenarios the viewer might be in. This shows deep understanding of their
situation and creates an "either way, I need this" feeling.

**Formula:**

> "Whether you're [scenario A — stuck / plateaued] or [scenario B — missing an opportunity
> entirely], we'll help you change that."

**Why two:** it casts a wider net while still feeling targeted. The viewer sees themselves in one
of them and feels understood.

**Example:**

> "Whether your brand is stuck at the same spend level watching efficiency decline on Google Ads,
> or you're Meta-only and missing out on an entire channel of high-intent buyers…"

Two. Not one, not four. One is too narrow to catch the room; four reads as a list and stops
feeling personal.

---

## 3. Founder authority — 30–60 seconds maximum

Establish credibility through **specialisation and results**, not lifestyle. Keep it tight.

**Include:**

- Name and company
- Years of specialisation — emphasise how **narrow** the focus is
- Aggregate results: $X revenue generated, X clients served
- Team composition: lean, senior, specific geography if relevant

**Do NOT include:**

- Personal lifestyle — cars, travel, penthouses
- Age flex
- A "how I got started" origin story — that belongs on the B2C route, not here

**Why narrow specialisation works:** "Not Meta, not TikTok, just Google" instantly differentiates
you from generalists. B2B buyers want specialists, not jacks-of-all-trades.

This is the section that most often runs long. If the draft is over 60 seconds of authority, cut
back to the specialisation line and the aggregate number.

---

## 4. Proof stack — 3–5 case studies

Rapid-fire client results. Each follows the same pattern but demonstrates a different angle.

**Pattern:**

> "[Company/Name] went from [specific before] to [specific after] — that's [impressive reframe
> of the number]."

**Vary the angle across the stack:**

| Angle | What it proves | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Scale story | You can take small to big | "$11K/mo to $222K/mo in spend — that's 22x growth" |
| Efficiency story | Same spend, better results | "Maintained 8x ROAS while scaling to $100K/mo" |
| Launch story | Zero to proven | "$0 to $387K in revenue with only $22K in ad spend" |
| Speed story | Fast results | "$50K–$100K in monthly revenue within the first 30 days" |

**The line that closes the stack:**

> "These aren't outliers. This is what happens when you apply [systematic approach] instead of
> [what everyone else does]."

`[SHOW DASHBOARD]` or `[SHOW RESULTS GRAPH]` beside every single result. A number with no visual
is a claim; a number with a screenshot is proof.

If you have fewer than three real results, write the placeholders and say so. Do not pad the stack
with invented clients.

---

## 5. Cost of inaction

Explain what *not* working with you costs them. Use logic and market dynamics, not fear.

**Three angles — use one or two, never all three:**

- **Competition capture:** "Your competitors are capturing the demand YOU created with your brand
  awareness campaigns."
- **Hidden waste:** "You're likely bleeding 60–80% of your budget to remarketing without
  realising it."
- **Widening gap:** "The gap between brands that scale profitably across channels and those that
  don't is widening every single quarter."

**Then bridge out of it:**

> "And here's the thing — [reframe that makes the solution feel easier than expected]."

Note: that bridge is the one place the phrase survives, and only if it genuinely adds energy at
that moment. If it does not, cut it — see the filler rule in `core-rules.md`.

---

## 6. Mechanism / system walkthrough

Name the system and walk through exactly what is included. **This is the longest section —
60–120 seconds.** Be specific enough to feel real, vague enough that they cannot DIY it.

**Structure it in three phases:**

1. **Foundation / setup** — audit, strategy, infrastructure
2. **Build / launch** — campaigns, creative, technical setup
3. **Optimise / scale** — ongoing management, testing, scaling

**Include proprietary elements:** custom AI, proprietary frameworks, unique methodology.
Something they cannot get anywhere else.

**Stage directions throughout this section:** `[SHOW DASHBOARD]`, `[SHOW ACCOUNT STRUCTURE]`,
`[SHOW RESULTS GRAPH]`.

The system needs a name. If the user has not given you one, write `[SYSTEM NAME]` and flag it in
the placeholder list — an unnamed mechanism is the single most common weakness in a B2B VSL, and
naming it is a decision for them, not for you.

---

## 7. Objection handling — 2–3 objections

Address objections directly, by name. B2B objections are not B2C objections.

| Objection | How to handle it |
|---|---|
| **"Why does it cost $X?"** | Break down what is included. Compare to the cost of doing it wrong. *"This isn't just a template, it's a completely bespoke system."* |
| **"How long until results?"** | A specific timeline with milestones. *"Day 7–14: first data. Day 30: clear visibility. Day 60: proven, scalable system."* |
| **"What if it doesn't work?"** | No hard guarantee — too many variables — but a strong confidence statement. *"I've never had to refund a client because of poor performance."* |
| **"I've been burned by agencies before"** | Acknowledge it, then differentiate: specialisation, senior team, no junior account managers. |

Use the objections the user actually gave you at Step 2. These four are the common ones, not a
substitute for asking.

---

## 8. Disqualification — who this is NOT for

Actively disqualify bad-fit viewers. This **increases** conversion from good-fit viewers, because
it builds trust and exclusivity.

**Formula:**

> "This is NOT for you if you're [disqualifier 1], [disqualifier 2], or [disqualifier 3]. We work
> with [qualifier] who understand that [reframe of what it takes]."

Do not soften it and do not skip it. A VSL that will take anyone reads as a VSL that needs anyone.

---

## 9. CTA + application frame

Drive to an **application**, not a purchase. Frame the application as a filter, not a sales pitch.

1. "Click the button below this video"
2. "Fill out a quick application — [what it asks for]"
3. "This helps us understand if we're a fit before we get on the call"
4. "If you're accepted, we'll schedule a strategy call where…"
5. "Our team has limited capacity because [real reason]"

**The key phrase:**

> "If we're not a fit, I'll tell you."

That single line builds more trust than anything else in the section. Keep it.

Step 5's capacity line must name a **real** reason. Capacity because the senior team only handles
N accounts is real. "Only 5 spots left" is not, and it is banned on this route.

---

## 10. Social proof recap + final push

Brief recap of results, restate the core promise, final CTA. No heavy emotional manipulation —
confidence and clarity.

**Formula:**

> "We've built a system that's helped [X] brands [result]. We'll install it in your business in
> [timeframe]. Click the button below, fill out the application, and let's talk."

Then stop. Do not add a second close, a countdown, or a "wait, one more thing". This route ends
on competence, not pressure.

---

## Section timing at a glance

For a 7-minute cut (~1,050 words). Scale proportionally for 5 or 10.

```
1.  Qualification gate + bold claim     0:00–0:20
2.  Problem — two scenarios             0:20–0:50
3.  Founder authority                   0:50–1:40    (30–60s, hard cap)
4.  Proof stack, 3–5 case studies       1:40–2:50
5.  Cost of inaction                    2:50–3:30
6.  Mechanism walkthrough               3:30–5:30    (longest section, 60–120s)
7.  Objection handling, 2–3             5:30–6:15
8.  Disqualification                    6:15–6:35
9.  CTA + application frame             6:35–6:55
10. Social proof recap + final push     6:55–7:10
```
