# B2B Format B — Case Study VSL

A structured breakdown of one client's transformation: the data, the strategy, and a testimonial
clip at the end. Best for retargeting or mid-funnel pages that show several case studies
alongside each other.

**8–15 minutes · ~1,200–2,250 words · 7 sections.**

Reference example from the SOP: the Viral Coach Dr. the founder case study
(`viralcoach.com/get-started/about-you`).

---

## When to pick this over Format A

- The page is **retargeting or mid-funnel**. The viewer already knows who you are; a second
  qualification gate wastes their time.
- You have **several case studies** to run side by side, so each video can go deep on one client
  rather than skimming five.
- The offer is complex enough that *how* you got the result is more persuasive than the result
  itself.

Format A is still the right choice for a cold-traffic landing page. A cold viewer will not sit
through someone else's story before you have earned it.

---

## One client, one video

Format B is about **one** client, start to finish. If the user wants three case studies, that is
three videos, not one video with three stories. Say so at the gate in Step 3.

Ask for that client's real before-state numbers, the real changes made and the real after-state
numbers. If any of the three is missing, the video has a hole in it that no amount of writing
fills — flag it rather than papering over it with a placeholder in the middle of the results
section.

---

## 1. Case study hook

Open with the result and the client.

> "This is how we [impressive result] for [client name/type]."

Result first, then the name. The number is what buys you the next thirty seconds.

`[SHOW RESULTS GRAPH]` or `[SHOW DASHBOARD]` on the opening claim.

---

## 2. The problem — the before state

What the client was struggling with before. **Be specific:** agency spending, metrics,
frustrations, what was not working.

Specific means numbers. "They were struggling with their ads" is nothing. "They were spending
$40K a month with an agency, sitting at a 1.8x ROAS, and had no idea which campaigns were
carrying it" is the before state.

Every one of those numbers must come from the user. `[BEFORE — SPEND]`, `[BEFORE — ROAS]` if not.

---

## 3. The diagnosis

What was actually wrong. Position yourself as the expert who saw what everyone else missed.

> "Despite having experience in the field, it didn't translate to expertise in [what actually
> matters]."

This is the section that separates a case study from a testimonial. The testimonial says it
worked. The diagnosis says **you understood why it wasn't working**, and that is what makes the
viewer believe you would understand their account too.

---

## 4. The fix — strategy applied

Walk through **3–5 specific changes** made. Enough detail to seem credible, not enough to
replicate.

The balance: name the change and the reasoning, withhold the execution. "We rebuilt the account
around search intent tiers instead of product categories, which meant the budget followed buyers
instead of browsers" is credible and not a how-to.

Stage directions land well here: `[SHOW ACCOUNT STRUCTURE]`, `[SHOW BEFORE/AFTER STRUCTURE]`.

---

## 5. The results — the after state

Specific metrics. Before-versus-after comparisons. Growth percentages. Revenue impact. Time
savings.

Mirror the exact metrics you used in section 2, in the same order. A before state measured in
ROAS and an after state measured in revenue is not a comparison, and a sharp viewer will notice.

`[SHOW DASHBOARD]` · `[SHOW REVENUE GRAPH]` · `[SHOW STRIPE]` against each figure.

---

## 6. Key insight / takeaway

**One** strategic principle the viewer can take away. This positions you as a thought leader, not
just a service provider.

One, not a list. It should be true beyond this client — something the viewer can apply on Monday
whether or not they ever hire you. Giving that away is what makes the video worth their eight
minutes, and it is the section people quote back to you on the call.

---

## 7. Client testimonial

End with the client speaking in their own words. **30–60 seconds** of them confirming the
experience and the results.

`[CUT TO CLIENT TESTIMONIAL CLIP — 30–60s]`

This is filmed footage, not scripted lines. Do not write words for the client to say. If the
footage does not exist yet, mark it clearly:

```
[TESTIMONIAL CLIP NEEDED — 30–60s of [CLIENT NAME] on camera confirming the result.
 If unavailable, close on section 6 and add a CTA card instead.]
```

---

## The CTA question

The seven sections do not include a call to action, because Format B usually sits on a page that
already has one — a booking widget, an application form, or a row of other case studies.

Ask the user where this video sits. If it is doing the closing work itself, add a short CTA after
section 7 in Format A's shape: click below, fill out the application, "if we're not a fit, I'll
tell you". Keep it to 15–20 seconds. Do not bolt Format A's full section 9 onto the end.

---

## Section timing at a glance

For an 11-minute cut (~1,650 words).

```
1. Case study hook               0:00–0:30
2. The problem — before state    0:30–2:00
3. The diagnosis                 2:00–3:30
4. The fix — strategy applied    3:30–6:30    (longest section)
5. The results — after state     6:30–8:30
6. Key insight / takeaway        8:30–9:45
7. Client testimonial            9:45–10:45   (30–60s of client footage)
   [optional CTA]                10:45–11:05
```
