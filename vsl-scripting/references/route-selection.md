# Route selection

Three frameworks live in this skill. This file is how you choose between them and what actually
changes once you have.

---

## The three routes

| Route | The offer | The buyer | The framework | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B2B** | A service, system or done-for-you engagement. Ads management, funnel builds, lead gen, agency retainers. | A business owner with revenue. | Format A (10 sections) or Format B (7 sections) | 5–10 min / 8–15 min |
| **B2C** | A mentorship, community, course, certification or coaching programme. Typically $2K–$7K with financing. | A consumer. Pre-revenue, aspiring, a beginner. | The 11 beats | 8–15 min |
| **Webinar** | The seat at an event. The programme is sold on the webinar, not in this video. | Either. | Doc B's compressed structure (10 sections, several skipped) | 3–5 min |

---

## How to choose

### Question 1 — who is buying?

**B2B tells:**

- Selling a service, system or done-for-you offer to business owners
- The CTA is "apply" or "book a call", not "buy now"
- The viewer is already running a business and has revenue
- Credibility comes from client results and portfolio, not lifestyle
- The tone is professional-confident, not hype-driven
- Qualification is a hard threshold: revenue, ad spend, team size

**B2C tells:**

- Selling info products, courses, certifications or coaching to consumers
- The audience is pre-revenue or aspiring — students, job-seekers, beginners
- Lifestyle proof is a primary credibility mechanism
- The founder's own origin story is load-bearing, not decoration
- The qualification question is financial capacity, not business size:
  *"do you have capital to invest in yourself right now?"*

### Question 2 — what is the click asking for?

If the click **registers them for an event**, the route is **webinar**, whoever is buying. The
video sells the seat. The webinar sells the offer, which is why the offer stack and the objection
handling compress or disappear from the script.

### The niche does not decide

"Trading education" is B2C. "Trading education sold to fund managers" is B2B. "A free training on
Google Ads for $1M ecom brands" is a webinar with a B2B tone. Ask about the buyer and the click,
never about the topic.

### Boundary cases, and what to do

| Situation | Route | Why |
|---|---|---|
| B2C programme, but the funnel runs through a webinar | **Webinar** | The video's only job is the registration. Take the B2C tone and the B2C proof types into the webinar structure. |
| B2B offer, but the click is a free training registration | **Webinar** | Same reason. Keep B2B proof — metrics, not lifestyle — and keep the scarcity honest (see below). |
| Coaching sold to business owners who already earn from the thing being taught | **B2B** | They have revenue and they buy on ROI. |
| Coaching sold to people who want to start earning from the thing being taught | **B2C** | Pre-revenue. They buy on belief. |
| A consumer offer that sells directly from the video, no call and no webinar | **Fallback** — see `references/webinar.md`, cold-traffic setting | None of the three routes was written for it. Say which you are using and why. |

## What actually changes between the routes

This is the whole reason the split exists. Get the route right and these fall out automatically;
get it wrong and every one of them is wrong at once.

### The opening beat

- **B2B** — a **qualification gate**, not a pattern interrupt. It is a filter, deliberately.
  *"If you're a business making over $1 million a year and you aren't already profitably scaling
  on Google Ads, here's what we'll do for you."* The wrong viewer should leave in five seconds.
- **B2C** — a **callout that names their exact situation and pain**. They should feel seen, not
  screened. *"You've watched hundreds of hours on YouTube, you know what a liquidity sweep is,
  but you're still inconsistent… sound familiar?"*
- **Webinar** — a **pattern interrupt / bold claim**. A specific result or statement, no warm-up,
  no introduction.

### The origin story

- **B2B** — **cut it.** Founder authority is 30–60 seconds of specialisation and aggregate
  results. No "how I got started".
- **B2C** — **beat 3, and one of the most important in the video.** Rags-to-results with real
  numbers and a real low point: zero → rock bottom → breakthrough → success. The story is the
  proof that an ordinary beginner can do this.
- **Webinar** — compressed to 30–60 seconds of authority. A relevant flex that earns the right
  to teach, not a biography.

### The proof

- **B2B** — 3–5 case studies. Revenue, ROAS, CAC, LTV, growth multiples. Vary the angle: scale
  story, efficiency story, launch story, speed story.
- **B2C** — 2–4 named student results with before / after / timeframe, plus reviews, on-screen
  payouts, testimonial clips woven through, plus a transparency play. Lifestyle proof belongs
  here and is expected.
- **Webinar** — 2–4 named results, same pattern, kept tight.

### Lifestyle

- **B2B** — banned. No cars, no travel, no penthouses, no age flex.
- **B2C** — expected. `[SHOW LIFESTYLE B-ROLL]` is a legitimate stage direction on this route.

### Scarcity and urgency

- **B2B** — **no manufactured scarcity.** "Limited capacity because we don't take on more clients
  than we can serve" beats "only 5 spots left". Urgency is a real constraint or it is absent.
- **B2C and webinar** — **a scarcity close is required.** It is a beat, not an optional flourish.
  It must still be real: a genuine date, a genuine cohort cap, genuine market timing, genuine
  saturation. Both sides of this skill agree that fake urgency is worthless — they disagree only
  on whether the beat is mandatory.

### The guarantee

- **B2B** — no hard guarantee. Too many variables. Use a strong confidence statement instead:
  *"I've never had to refund a client because of poor performance."*
- **B2C** — a **conditional risk reversal near the end**, backed by a believable stat. Never lead
  with it. The guarantee supports the offer; it is not the offer.
- **Webinar** — usually absent. The webinar handles it.

### The call to action

- **B2B** — apply, not buy. The application is framed as a filter.
  *"If we're not a fit, I'll tell you."*
- **B2C** — CTA to apply, then the risk reversal, then a **second CTA with an identity
  challenge**. Two CTAs, in that order.
- **Webinar** — register. Clear mechanical instructions: click below, enter name / email / phone
  correctly. Close with a forward-looking line.

### Objections

- **B2B** — 2–3, addressed by name. Price, timeline, "I've been burned by agencies before".
- **B2C** — pre-empt the one big objection, which is almost always *"I've tried courses before"*.
  The answer is the difference in mechanism, not more content.
- **Webinar** — skip or compress. The webinar does that work.

### Tone

- **B2B** — professional-confident. Never desperate, never over-the-top. Default to
  professional-casual unless their existing content sits elsewhere.
- **B2C** — warm, first-person, relatable. The founder is someone who was recently where the
  viewer is now.
- Both — match tonality from the client's existing content. The script sounds like them.

## History: why this skill has three routes and not one

Worth knowing, because older documents still point at the broken arrangement.

The B2B document ended its routing section by telling anyone with a B2C offer to "use the B2C
`vsl-scripting` skill". No such skill existed. The document that *was* published as
`vsl-scripting` — and that the B2C SOP linked by that name — covered webinar registration and
cold traffic. It contained no B2C biz-opp content and had never heard of the 11-beat framework
that the B2C SOP itself specifies for the VSL.

So a B2C operator following the documentation exactly arrived at a skill that could not write
the video their own SOP told them to make.

All three frameworks now live in this one skill and are selected explicitly at Step 1. When a SOP
or an older document says "use the B2C VSL skill", it means `references/b2c-framework.md`.
