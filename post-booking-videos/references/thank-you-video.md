# The Thank You video (the confirmation video)

The first and most important video on the page. It does five jobs at once: gets them to
complete the confirmation steps, builds authority, proves results, sells the process, and
pushes them into the rest of the page.

> **Treat it as a second sales pitch, not a "you're booked" clip.** This is the mistake that
> costs the most. A 40-second "thanks, see you Tuesday" video does none of the four jobs
> after the first one.

**Length: 8–15 minutes.** The reference page's runs about **12 minutes**. It is long on
purpose, because it is doing real selling. Do not let a user talk you down to two minutes
"because attention spans" — the people watching this have already booked a call with them.

---

## 1. The seven beats — non-negotiable

Every one of these, in this order, or the video is not finished. This is the checklist the
finished script is graded against.

| # | Beat | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Congratulate them for taking action | They just did something most people don't. Opening on approval, not admin, buys the next ten minutes of attention |
| 2 | Go to your email, check spam, accept the calendar invite — or the meeting gets cancelled | The two confirm actions, and the stakes that make them happen. This beat alone is most of the show-up rate |
| 3 | Commit to the time: quiet place, at a desk, not in the car | A call taken in a car does not close. Setting the environment now is what makes the call worth turning up to |
| 4 | Come with questions | Turns a passive attendee into a participant, and it pre-loads the call with the things actually blocking the sale |
| 5 | What the call actually covers | Removes "what even is this?" — the reason a booked lead quietly decides it isn't worth the hour |
| 6 | No reschedules, and the cancellation fee for no-shows | Cost of not showing. A slot that costs nothing to skip gets skipped |
| 7 | Scroll down — there's more | Without it the page's Q&A, breakdowns and proof are never seen, and the pre-sell never happens |

**Beats 4 and 6 land in the close**, alongside the restated steps — see the running order
below. Do not bolt them on as an eighth section at the end.

### Beat 6 needs a real number

The SOP states a **$75 cancellation fee**. Use the user's own figure if they gave one; if
they skipped it, write `[$XX cancellation fee]` and tell them plainly: a no-reschedule policy
with no number attached changes nobody's behaviour.

### B2C only — the phone-number expectation beat

**B2C adds one beat the B2B script does not have.** Their setter is going to ring, and B2C
leads do not answer unknown numbers. Say it in the video so the call gets picked up. It sits
with beat 3, as part of setting up how the call works.

The SOP's wording:

```
The team calls or texts from a 305 or 786 number, and it may show as a
local city number for compliance and to reach people internationally.
Don't be confused.
```

Swap those area codes for theirs. **B2B does not need this line** — an operator expecting a
business call answers the phone. Mark it clearly as B2C-only in the finished script so a B2B
user doesn't read it out.

When a B2C funnel's booked-to-show number is low, the SOP's own diagnosis order is: setter
speed, **this phone-number note**, then the pre-call SMS. It is a named fix, not a nicety.

## 2. The full running order

The seven beats are the minimum. This is the 8–15 minute version that also sells. Write the
script against this order, with the beat numbers marked in the margin so they can check them
off while filming.

1. **Open on the stakes and the confirmation ask.** *(beats 1 + 2)*
   Congratulate them first, then name the risk in the first breath:
   *"Your call is NOT confirmed yet, and unless you do one thing right now, we'll have to
   cancel it."*

2. **Give the two steps by name.** *(beat 2)*
   - Step one: *"Go to your email and click **Yes, I'm attending** on the email from
     [name / your business partner]."*
   - Step two: *"Accept the calendar invite from [name / your client-success lead] to lock in
     the time."*
   - Then say what it proves: *"This tells us you're committed and you're actually coming."*

   Those two names are real people. They must match the names in steps 1 and 2 on the page
   itself, and those emails have to actually get sent.

3. **Handle "I can't find it" before it happens.** *(beat 2)*
   *"If you don't see the emails, check spam or promotions, and if you still can't find them,
   DM me here: [handle]."* Most failed confirmations are a filtered email, not a change of
   heart.

4. **Give the pause command.**
   *"Pause this video right now and do that before we continue."* Then stop talking for a
   beat. This is what gets the confirm actions done **while intent is at its highest** — five
   minutes later they are back in their day and it never happens.

5. **Re-introduce yourself and build authority.**
   Who they are, their short story, their own results with specific numbers — revenue, months
   hit, converted into the viewer's local currency where it helps. Then total client results:
   *"$X generated for clients over Y years."* Never write a number the user did not give you.

6. **Do the live proof walkthrough.**
   Screen-share real, unpolished proof — the client Discord, the CRM, the wins channel — and
   scroll through win after win. Names, cash collected, before-to-after. Convert currency
   where it helps. Signal abundance out loud: *"I could keep scrolling for an hour."*

   **This is the part that makes them believe it is real,** and it is the single most
   persuasive thing on the whole page. A polished results reel reads as an advert; a messy
   scroll through a live channel reads as true. Script it as a screen-share instruction, not
   as words.

7. **Transition to the process.**
   *"It's one thing to show you the results, let me show you exactly HOW we get them."*

8. **Sell the mechanism.**
   Their system, step by step, in plain words. Contrast it against the alternative the viewer
   would otherwise consider and say why theirs is better, cheaper or faster. If they have
   unit economics — cost per lead, per booking, per close, against the other way — show them.
   This is what separates them from everyone else in the market.

9. **Guarantee and scarcity.**
   The risk reversal: *"I guarantee [result] in [timeframe] or we work for free."* Then real
   scarcity: *"we're only taking on [X] more [clients] in [place]."* **Real** scarcity. An
   invented number here is the one that gets repeated on camera and then contradicted on the
   call.

10. **Restate the steps, prep them, close.** *(beats 3, 4, 5, 6, 7)*
    - Send them back to accept the email and the calendar invite.
    - How to show up: on time, at a desk, pen and pad, ready to take notes — **quiet place,
      not in the car**. *(beat 3)*
    - **With their questions written down.** *(beat 4)*
    - **What the call actually covers** — the agenda, in one or two sentences. *(beat 5)*
    - **The no-reschedule policy and the cancellation fee**, said as a number. *(beat 6)*
    - *(B2C)* the phone-number expectation.
    - Remove the risk of the call itself: *"there's no downside to the call, only upside."*
    - Push them to act before someone takes the spot.
    - **Tell them to keep scrolling — there's more below.** *(beat 7)*
    - End on *"I'll see you on the call."*

> Why it works: the steps, stakes and commitment language secure the show, while the
> authority, proof, process and guarantee pre-sell. People arrive already sold, and a portion
> pay before the call.

## 3. What sits around it on the page

Not this skill's job to build, but the script has to match it.

Above the video, the page opens blunt on purpose:

```
Your Strategy Call is NOT Confirmed Yet — watch the video below and
complete all the steps to confirm.
```

It reads harsh. That urgency is what drives the confirm actions and lifts the show-up rate.
Under the video: **"We'll ask if you watched this before we start."**

The script's opening line has to agree with that headline. If the page says NOT CONFIRMED and
the video opens with "you're all booked in", the whole mechanism collapses.

---

## 4. Shot list for this one video

- **Landscape.** It runs in a wide player and it carries a screen-share.
- **Founder to camera**, phone propped at eye level, real office or desk.
- **Quiet room.** Wired earbuds with a mic beat a bare phone with an echo.
- **Two segments, one take each** if a single 12-minute take is too much: everything up to
  the pause command, then the rest. Do not cut it any finer than that.
- **Screen-record beat 6 separately if easier** — the wins channel scroll — and hand both
  files to whoever assembles it. If they are editing nothing, do it live.
- **Have open before recording:** their inbox (to show the confirmation email), their calendar
  invite, and their wins channel / CRM.
- Upload **unlisted to YouTube or straight to Loom**, copy the normal share link.

## 5. Failure modes to check the script against

- A 40-second "thanks, see you Tuesday" clip. Short is the biggest reason this page
  underperforms.
- **No pause command.** They watch the whole video and never accept the invite, and the
  best-intentioned leads still get cancelled.
- Proof described instead of shown. Beat 6 is a screen-share, not a sentence.
- Beat 7 missing. Everything below the video goes unwatched and the pre-sell never runs.
- A cancellation policy stated with no number in it.
- B2C script missing the phone-number beat, then a setter wondering why nobody answers.
